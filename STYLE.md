# SEA motion style

Based on SEA Design System v1.1 and its approved educational slides.

- Cream #F4F1EA for explanations; navy #0B2545 for chapter cards.
- Green #1F7A4D for emphasis; orange #F79B4B for results; sage #9AC3AF for dividers.
- IBM Plex Sans Arabic, regular 400 and bold 700. Arabic RTL; mathematical expressions LTR.
- Original transparent logo: light and dark variants included. No new box around it.
- Minimal layouts, generous whitespace, one concept per scene.
- Reveal one meaningful group at a time, about 0.45 seconds for the entrance. Hold while learners read.
- Time motion to the instructor's real spoken explanation.
- Do not split Arabic words into independently animated letters.
- Frame: 1920×1080, 30fps.
- If source slides/layouts are outside SEA's identity, rebuild the entire visual track using SEA scenes. Keep only the original instructor audio in the output. The source picture is a content reference, not a finished background.
- Recreate figures, labels, numerical values and equations faithfully. Show relationships and processes progressively; do not reduce every explanation to text bullets.
- If preserving already-compatible source footage or an explicitly approved software-capture exception, use contain framing so program controls remain visible.
- Focus coordinates are percentages of the final frame, including letterboxing where applicable.
- Focus/callout over a rebuilt lesson must sit above a SEA-authored visual, not an old-slide screenshot.
- No random decorative motion, flashing, exaggerated bounce or constant zoom.
- Rebuilt visuals must cover the entire retained audio; no blank gaps, demo-slide fallback or mismatched source-slide flashes.

## Sound effects

- Include short SFX when they reinforce a meaningful event: soft click/tick for a deliberate reveal, gentle whoosh for a scene change, restrained chime for a result.
- Aim for about 0.1–0.5 seconds; use a longer tail only when it remains unobtrusive and useful.
- Use one sound for a meaningful group, not each letter, word or tiny movement. Silent moments are welcome.
- Keep effects distinctly below speech; start about 18–24 dB below the instructor's typical speech level, then judge by listening. A numeric gain is only a starting point because asset levels vary.
- Use short fades to avoid clicks, avoid overlapping effects, and trim excessive tails. Check the combined mix for clipping.
- Keep speech clear and its level consistent. Reduce or move an effect when it masks a syllable.
- Avoid loud impacts, meme sounds, harsh alerts, long risers and continuous sound beds.
- Original instructor speech remains the primary audio; music and replacement narration require an explicit request.
